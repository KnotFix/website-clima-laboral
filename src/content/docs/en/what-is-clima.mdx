Clima is a tool for **measuring workplace climate by segment**. Not for averaging the
company into a single number: for being able to say the Northern Region is eight
points down, that the problem is concentrated in night shifts, and that it has been
that way for two years.

## The problem it solves

Most climate surveys end in a number and a PDF. The number does not tell you where to
act —a whole company averaged has no «where»— and the PDF cannot be asked a second
question.

Clima is built around the part that normally gets lost: **the analysis afterwards**.
Results break down by any branch of your organization, cross with demographic axes,
compare against each other, and carry over time.

## The three pieces

**An org tree you build yourself.** There are no fixed levels: you model your company
as it is, at whatever depth it has. Everything else —employees, questionnaires,
permissions, results— hangs off that tree. See [The org tree](/en/docs/org-tree).

**An instrument in two parts.** A common core of questions, which is what makes
results comparable across areas and across years, plus the questions you attach to
each branch. One store can have its own three questions and the rest of the company
never sees them.

**Two indices.** Satisfaction, which measures how content people are with the aspects
of their work, and Climate, which measures how the environment is perceived as a whole.
Two different questions about the same people, and they are not compared against each
other. See [Satisfaction and Climate](/en/docs/indices).

## How people respond

Two ways, both anonymous:

- **Kiosk.** A tablet at the workplace, with several stations if needed. This is what
  works when much of your workforce has no corporate email and no computer.
- **Email.** Each person receives a single-use link.

## What it guarantees about anonymity

This is not a marketing promise, it is how the database is built: **who responded and
what they responded are stored with no relationship between them**. The product can
tell you Juan has responded —you need that to chase participation— and it cannot tell
you what he answered. Not you, and not us.

On top of that there is a **confidentiality threshold**: results for a group that is
too small are not published, because a result from three people is anonymous in
theory and not in practice. See [Anonymity and minimum N](/en/docs/anonymity).

> **This is worth understanding before you start, not after.** It is what you will
> have to explain to your people so they answer honestly, and it is also the reason a
> result you expected to see sometimes comes out hidden.

## What you have when a study ends

- Both indices, for every node in the tree that gathers enough responses.
- The breakdown by category, and question by question.
- Segments you build by hand, crossing branch with sex, age band and tenure.
- The comparison against your previous studies, and the period's improvement targets.
- All of it downloadable as Excel, PDF or a presentation.
