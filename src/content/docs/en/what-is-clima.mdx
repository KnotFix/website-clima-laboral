Clima is a tool for the **measurement of workplace climate by segment**. Its purpose is
not to produce a single organization-wide average, but to permit precise and actionable
statements: that the Northern Region stands eight points below the rest, that the
incidence is concentrated in night shifts, and that the situation has persisted for two
years.

## The problem it solves

Most climate surveys conclude in a single indicator and a PDF report. The indicator does
not allow the organization to identify where to intervene: the average of an entire
organization does not locate the problem. The report, for its part, admits no further
enquiry of the data.

Clima is designed around the stage that is habitually lost: **the subsequent analysis**.
Results are disaggregated by any branch of the organizational structure, crossed with
the demographic axes, compared against one another and monitored over time.

## The three components

**An organizational tree defined by the organization itself.** There are no
predetermined levels: the structure is modelled in accordance with the reality of the
company, at whatever depth applies in each case. Employees, questionnaires,
permissions and results are all organized from that tree. See
[The org tree](/en/docs/org-tree).

**An instrument in two parts.** A common core of questions, which is what guarantees the
comparability of results across areas and across periods, and a set of specific
questions associated with each branch. A branch office may incorporate three questions
of its own without the rest of the organization having access to them.

**Two indices.** Satisfaction, which measures the degree of contentment of personnel
with the various aspects of their work, and Climate, which measures the perception of
the working environment as a whole. They constitute two different measurements of the
same population and are not comparable with each other. See
[Satisfaction and Climate](/en/docs/indices).

## Modes of response

There are two modes, both anonymous:

- **Kiosk.** A tablet placed at the workplace, with the option of enabling several
  stations. It is particularly suitable where a significant portion of the personnel has
  neither corporate email nor computer equipment.
- **Email.** Each person receives a single-use link.

## Anonymity guarantees

This is not a commercial commitment but a characteristic of the database design: **the
record of who responded and the record of what was responded are stored with no link
whatsoever between them**. The system can report that a given person has already
participated, a datum necessary for the management of participation, but cannot report
the content of their responses. This restriction extends to the totality of users, the
provider included.

To this is added a **confidentiality threshold**: the results of a group that does not
reach a minimum number of responses are not published, given that a result based on
three people is anonymous in formal terms but not in practice. See
[Anonymity and minimum N](/en/docs/anonymity).

> **These criteria are best understood before the study begins and not once it has
> concluded.** They constitute the explanation that will have to be conveyed to
> personnel in order to encourage candid responses, and they are likewise the reason
> certain expected results may appear hidden.

## Results available once a study concludes

- Both indices, for every section of the tree that gathers a sufficient number of
  responses.
- The disaggregation by category and the question-by-question detail.
- Manually constructed segments, combining branch with sex, age band and tenure.
- The comparison with previous studies and the improvement targets for the period.
- The export of all the information in Excel, PDF or presentation format.
