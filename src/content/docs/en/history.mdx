From the second study onward, Clima stops showing you a photograph and starts showing
you a film. The Statistics module cuts across studies: it compares between them, so it
does not live inside any one of them.

## What you can follow over time

- **The indices**, each with its own line.
- **The categories**, to see whether the one you attacked last year moved.
- **A single question**, with its acceptance percentage and the distribution of
  responses study by study.
- **The targets**: what you set against what came out.

## Gaps are information

When a point in the series does not clear the confidentiality threshold, **it is drawn
as a gap and the line does not cross it**.

Joining the two neighbouring points and moving on would look tidier. It would also be a
lie: that straight line would assert a trend across a period nothing is known about.
The gap says exactly what happened —there was no publishable data there— and that is a
fact in itself.

The same goes for periods with no study. An empty row is not a zero.

## When a question «breaks the series»

If a question changes **what it asks**, its previous results stop being comparable with
the new ones. The line splits into two segments that do not join.

The question stays alive and the old responses are not lost. What is lost is the right
to draw a trend between the two halves, because they are not the same question.

> **Fixing a typo should not break anything — but fixing it while a study is collecting
> is a different problem.** If you need to correct wording, wait for the study to close.
> See [Questionnaires and inheritance](/en/docs/questionnaires).

## What ends a series without looking like it

Three changes feel administrative and are actually instrument changes. Worth knowing
before you make them rather than after looking at the chart:

**Changing theoretical model.** The most expensive one. That index's history restarts
from zero, because they are two different instruments. See
[Theoretical models](/en/docs/models).

**Reorganizing the tree.** Results are published per node. If you merge two branches or
split one into three, the new nodes have no past: the old branch's series exists, but it
is not any of the new ones'.

**Changing the confidentiality threshold.** It rewrites nothing backwards —each study
freezes its own— but from now on points are published against a different bar. A series
where half the points were measured under one threshold and half under another is not
wrong, but you need to know it when reading.

## Comparing against other companies

Cross-organization comparison exists because the fixed sections are identical for
everyone — but only **among those measuring with the same model**. Two companies on
different models are not comparable, for the same reason you are not comparable with
yourself after switching.

## One reading tip

Two points are not a trend. With two studies, all you know is that the second came out
different from the first, and the possible causes include the survey going out at a
different time of year, or different people answering.

A series starts saying something around the third point. Until then, treat each study as
a photograph.
