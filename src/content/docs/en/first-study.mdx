Four things, in this order. **It is not a suggestion** — each one needs the previous:
without a tree there is nowhere to put employees, and without nodes there is nothing
to assign a questionnaire to. That is why the first three live together as tabs of
Configuration instead of being scattered across the menu.

## 1. The tree

You create your **root node** —with whatever name and type you want— and hang the
structure off it. The organization itself never appears in the tree: you place the
root.

Before subdividing much, read [The org tree](/en/docs/org-tree): the shape you give it
here decides what you will be able to compare later, and branches with very few
people produce results that will never be publishable.

## 2. The roster

Each person goes in a node. You add them one by one, or **import from Excel**, which
is the sane option above twenty people.

The import happens in three beats —**analyze, preview, and only then execute**— so
you see what is going to happen before it happens: how many rows go in, which ones
have problems, and what will be created.

<Shot
  src="nomina-importar.png"
  alt="The roster import wizard at its first step: mapping each spreadsheet column to its field, with a sample of the rows that were read."
  caption="Nothing is saved until the last step: mapping and review happen before that."

/>

> **The roster decides who gets asked**, so it is worth having it current before you
> launch. It is also what your plan's headcount allowance is counted against (see
> [Account and plan](/en/docs/account-and-plan)).

## 3. The questionnaires

The questionnaire has the **common core** —the questions that make results comparable
across areas and across years— plus whatever **own questions** you add.

It is assigned to a node and **all of its descendants inherit it**. That is what
saves you from assigning it store by store: attach it to the root and the whole
company has it; later, if one branch needs something different, you assign it its own
and that branch stops inheriting.

<Shot
  src="boletas-editor.png"

  caption="The first two sections are identical across every account measuring with the same model; the third one is yours."
  ratio="1377 / 1303"
/>

You can add questions of several types, including **conditional** ones: a yes/no that,
depending on the answer, opens a follow-up question, and that one another.

## 4. The study

With the tree, the people and the questionnaire in place, the study is the easy part.
A four-step wizard:

1. **Information** — the study's name and dates.
2. **Scope** — which node gets measured (and with it, everything below), and at what
   **confidence level**, which is what determines the sample size.
3. **Model** — which theoretical model measures Satisfaction. It arrives preloaded
   with the one in force on that branch's questionnaire, so normally you confirm and
   move on. See [Theoretical models](/en/docs/models).
4. **Review** — the summary of all three decisions before confirming.

<Shot
  src="estudio-wizard.png"
  alt="The study wizard at the scope step: the tree to pick the node, the summary with the eligible population, and the minimum sample calculation."
  caption="Step 2 of 4. Picking the node computes the minimum sample for that scope."
  ratio="1381 / 1235"
/>

On confirmation the **sample** is generated: not a random draw of names, but
**stratified sampling**, which is what keeps a segment from being under-represented.
The final screen shows you the actual sample that came out.

> **On launch, the study freezes.** Scope, questionnaire and confidentiality threshold
> are fixed until it closes. This is not a limitation: if the first half of your people
> answer one questionnaire and the second half another, both groups get published
> together as if they were one, and the result means nothing.
>
> While it is a **draft** everything can change. After launch, nothing.

## What comes after

When collection ends, you finalize the study and that is when results are computed and
stored. From then on you have both indices, the breakdown by branch and by category,
the segments, and —from the second study onward— the historical series.

## Two things before you launch

**Tell your people.** A survey that arrives with no context gets poor participation,
and participation is what decides how many results you will be able to publish.

**Check the threshold.** If your tree has small branches, look at the number before
measuring rather than after half the results come out hidden. See
[Anonymity and minimum N](/en/docs/anonymity).
