The **kiosk** means responding from a tablet at the workplace. It exists for a concrete
reason: in most organizations that measure climate, the majority of people **have no
corporate email and no computer**. A survey that only arrives by mail measures desk
workers.

## How it works, in one sentence

You open a **station** for each tablet, you **check in** whoever sits down, they
respond, and the station frees itself for the next person.

## Setting up the session

**1. Open one station per tablet.** Each gives you a link —or a QR— that you open
**once** on that tablet. That link is what turns the device into a station for this
study.

**2. Leave the tablet on that screen.** Nobody needs to sign in on it; the tablet has no
account and no user. It is a capture device, not a workstation.

**3. Run the queue.** When someone sits down, you **check them in** from the panel. The
tablet loads their questionnaire. When they finish, the station frees itself and is ready
for the next person.

> **Check-in is done by the operator, not by the person responding.** That is what stops
> anyone from responding twice or responding for someone else, and what lets forty people
> pass through one tablet in a morning without any of them seeing anything from the
> previous one.

## Several tablets at once

You can open as many stations as you have tablets, and the panel shows them all: which
is free, which is busy, and who is responding at each.

**Each session is yours.** If two HR people are surveying different branches, each sees
their own tablets. They do not collide and cannot shut down each other's stations.

### Station states

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Available** | Ready for the next check-in |
| **Busy** | Someone is responding right now |
| **Disconnected** | The tablet has not reported in for a while |

**«Disconnected» breaks nothing.** It is a presence indicator: it tells you the tablet
was switched off, ran out of battery or lost wifi. Nothing expires because of it, and
closing the tablet's tab does not unregister the device. When it comes back, it keeps
working.

## Mixed mode: kiosk and email together

It is not all or nothing. You can be running the kiosk session and, for one person who
did not show up, **send them the email invitation without taking them out of the
kiosk**.

That is the sane setup for a mixed organization: the plant responds on tablets and the
ten people in admin by email, in the same study.

## Anonymity during capture

Worth stating plainly, because this is where people doubt most: **the kiosk does not
weaken anonymity**.

The system knows a person's turn came up —that is participation, and it is what lets you
know who is left— and it stores their response separately, with no link between the two.
The operator doing check-in **does not see what gets answered**, and the tablet keeps
nothing from the previous person.

See [Anonymity and minimum N](/en/docs/anonymity).

> **The fragile part is not technical, it is physical.** A tablet against a wall with its
> screen facing the queue ruins perceived anonymity however impeccable the system is.
> Point it at a wall, not at the aisle — and **keep managers away**.

## When something goes wrong

**Someone walked off mid-survey.** Close the station and reopen it. That person stays
unanswered, and you can check them in again later.

**A tablet ran out of battery.** The session is not lost: that station shows as
disconnected and the rest keep working. With the tablet back, you open the link again.

**You lost a station's link.** It can be viewed again from the panel; you do not need to
create a new station.

**The session is over.** Close the stations. There is an action to close them all at
once, and another to delete temporary devices you will not use again.

## Before the first session

**Test with one tablet and one person** before forming a queue. The flow is simple, but
the first time is worth seeing without twenty people waiting.

**Do the arithmetic.** Multiply how long one response takes by the people in that branch
and divide by the number of tablets. The number tends to be larger than expected, and it
is what tells you how many tablets to bring.

**Announce it first.** See [Announcing the survey](/en/docs/announcing).
