Questions in **Section 3** —yours— can be of six types. The first three are scales and
are the ones that produce an acceptance percentage; the other three are read
differently.

## The six

| Type | What it is | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| `escala_1_4` | The instrument's scale, 1 to 4 | Nada · Algo · Mucho · Completamente |
| `escala_3` | Three-point scale | 1 · 2 · 3 (no words) |
| `si_no` | Yes/no | No · Sí |
| `opcion_multiple` | Choose among options you define | — |
| `ranking` | Order or pick several options | — |
| `abierta` | Free text | — |

### Why `escala_3` uses numbers

The verbal anchors of `escala_1_4` are verbatim from the official instrument. For three
points no wording is defined, and **inventing it would be inventing instrument**: the
words on a scale assert something about the distances between its values. So the numbers
are shown instead.

## Which ones produce an acceptance percentage

The three scale types —`escala_1_4`, `escala_3` and `si_no`— produce an **acceptance
percentage**: what proportion of responses fell on the favourable side. It is the
number you will see most often and the only one comparable over time.

Option, ranking and open questions **do not have one** —there is no order among their
answers— so they are read by their distribution or their content.

See [Reading a result](/en/docs/reading-results).

## How each is charted

**Scales and `si_no`** — A distribution bar with the spread of responses, plus the
acceptance percentage. In the historical view, a line of that percentage over time.

**Multiple choice** — The spread across options. Adds up to 100 %.

**Ranking** — **Counts, never percentages.** Its selection is multiple, so counts add up
to more than the responses: normalizing to 100 % would draw a distribution that does not
exist. That is why ranking stays out of distribution charts and is read in the table.

**Open** — Not charted. Read.

## Conditional blocks

Any question can have **children**: questions that only appear depending on how the
parent was answered. Most useful with yes/no questions.

```
Are you satisfied with communication in your area?   → No
   What is the main problem?                         → Lack of information
      What information is missing?                   → [text]
```

Nesting is **not capped at one level**: a block can go three or more deep, and every
level is captured and published.

> **Deleting a question takes all its descendants, at any depth.** Before you confirm,
> the product counts them and tells you how many — from the list it is not obvious,
> because children appear as ordinary questions.

## Which type to choose

**If you want to follow it over time, use a scale.** It is the only thing that produces
a number comparable year over year.

**If you want to understand a why, use a conditional block.** A yes/no that opens a
reason question gives you the metric *and* the explanation, and only shows up for people
who have something to say.

**Use `abierta` sparingly.** It is not charted and has to be read one by one. As the
closing question of a conditional block it works very well; as a standalone question to
the whole company it produces a thousand paragraphs nobody will process.

## Before adding questions

Remember that Section 3 questions are yours but **not free**: they lengthen the
questionnaire, and a long questionnaire lowers participation — which is what decides how
many results you get to publish. See [Announcing the survey](/en/docs/announcing).
