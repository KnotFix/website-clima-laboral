Every plan includes **every feature of the product**. What changes from one to the next is
**how many people you measure and how many studies you launch per year**.

That is not this page simplifying: there is no screen, no report and no feature sold
separately. The plan does not decide what you can do, it decides at what size.

## The 30-day trial

Every account is born in trial, **with no credit card**.

For those 30 days you can **configure everything**: build the tree, import the roster,
compose the questionnaires, assign them by branch and generate the sample. **No cap
applies**, so if your company has 800 people you can load all 800 and see the product
with your real data, not with a demo.

The one thing the trial does not do is **open collection**. Launching a study requires an
active plan.

> **That is deliberate, and it is the only line you cross by paying.** All the
> configuration work —which is the part that costs you— is done for free, and your draft
> stays saved exactly as it is. When you subscribe, you launch.

## The three axes

Only three things have a cap, and they are the three that cost someone money:

- **Studies per period** — how many times you open collection in the contracted year.
- **People on the roster** — how many active people you have loaded.
- **Email invitations per period** — how many emails go out.

**Everything else is uncapped.** Questionnaires, per-branch variants, your own Section 3
questions, kiosk stations, segments, reports, platform users, languages: unlimited on
every plan. Kiosk stations in particular are never charged —the tablets are yours— and
capping questionnaires capped nothing, because a questionnaire can be duplicated.

## What gets counted, and when it resets

**A «period» is your contracted year, not the calendar year.** Subscribe in March and your
period runs March to March, and March is when the counters go back to zero. The
distinction matters: there is no January 1st handing studies back to anyone.

**Studies are counted on launch, and the credit is not returned.** A finished study
consumed its credit, and that is right: closing it does not undo the collection.

**The roster counts active people.** Anyone on holiday or on leave **does not count**
toward the cap — it is the same criterion the product uses to build the sample, so the
number we sold you is the same number you can survey. Deactivating someone who has left
frees their place.

**Emails count sends, not people.** Re-inviting someone sends another email and adds
another. The kiosk consumes nothing on this axis: only email costs email.

All of it is visible on the plan screen, with its meter: what you have used and what you
have.

## If you go over the included roster

The people cap **is not a wall**. Above what is included you can keep adding people and
it is charged per head, up to a ceiling:

| | Included | Additional | Up to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | 100 | USD 30 per person per year | 150 |
| **Professional** | 500 | USD 15 per person per year | 750 |
| **Corporate** | 2,000 | USD 10 per person per year | 3,000 |

**Past the ceiling you have to move up a plan.** And the ceiling sits where it sits for a
reason: using a plan's full overage always costs less than the next plan, so it is never
in your interest to sit paying extras when the jump would already suit you better.

The overage is **billed separately** from the annual charge. The product tells you how
many people are over and what they add up to before it appears on any invoice, and the
roster importer warns you **in the preview**, not after you have mapped the columns.

## Account states

| State | Configure | Launch | Read results |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In trial** | Yes, uncapped | No | Yes |
| **Active** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Payment pending** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Read-only** | No | No | **Yes** |

**Payment pending** is the 14-day window that follows a charge that did not go through.
Everything works normally; it is a prompt to check the payment method, not a punishment.
Most failed payments are an expired card rather than a decision to leave, and cutting
access on day one would treat the two the same.

## «Read-only» deletes nothing

When the trial ends without a subscription, or when the subscription lapses, the account
goes read-only. And that means exactly what it says:

> **Your results and your history stay available to consult and download, forever.** They
> are not deleted, not degraded and not hidden behind a plan.

What you cannot do is **configure or launch new studies** until you reactivate. What you
already measured is already yours — and a climate history that evaporates when an invoice
lapses would not serve the one purpose a history serves.

## Changing plan

**Moving up** is immediate, at any time.

**Moving down** is allowed, on one condition: the new plan has to be enough for what you
already have loaded. A company that shrank its headcount moves down without trouble; one
with 600 active people cannot move down to a plan that admits 150, because it would be
over its own cap the same day it paid.

**The block happens before charging.** If your usage does not fit, the product tells you
by how much and charges you nothing.

## Cancelling

Cancelling means **«do not renew me»**, not «keep what I paid».

Your account **stays active until the period you already paid for ends**, with everything
working: you can launch, run and close studies right up to the last day. When that day
comes it goes read-only — and your results are still there.

Cancelling does not entitle you to the payment-pending window: that exists for the charge
that failed, and a cancellation is not a failure, it is a decision with a date of its own.

## How payment works

**A year in advance.** There is no monthly plan, and that is not an omission: a climate
study is annual, and a monthly one would let someone pay for one month, launch their study
for the year and leave.

Payment is processed by **ONVOPay**. **Your card details never pass through our servers**
at any point: the charge happens against the provider, directly from your browser.
