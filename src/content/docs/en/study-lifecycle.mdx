A study goes through **three states**, and each one allows something different. Almost
every time the product will not let you touch something, the answer is here.

## Draft

Every study is born here, and in this state **everything can change**: the name, the
dates, the scope, the model. The wizard resumes where you left it, and a draft can be
re-edited in full through all four steps, not just its name.

A draft asks nobody anything. It does not exist for employees.

## Launching

The transition from draft to in progress. Two things are required: the **sample must
already be generated**, and your plan must allow it (see
[Account and plan](/en/docs/account-and-plan)).

**On launch, three things freeze until the study closes:**

- **The scope.** Which node is measured and, with it, everything below.
- **The instrument.** Each branch's questionnaire and its questions. You also cannot
  reassign the questionnaire of a branch this study is measuring.
- **The confidentiality threshold.** The one in force is locked in. Changing it later
  does not touch this study.

At that same moment the period's **targets are set**. See
[Improvement targets](/en/docs/targets).

> **The freeze is not administrative rigidity: it is what makes the result mean
> something.** If the questionnaire changes halfway, the first half of your people
> answered one thing and the second half another, and both groups get published
> together as if they were one.
>
> Practical corollary: **change anything you want to change while it is a draft.** After
> launch, the only way out is waiting for it to close.

## In progress

The study is collecting. Here is the part that surprises people most:

> **A study in progress shows NO results. Only progress.**

They are not hidden by the threshold: they are not computed. Results are produced on
finalization, and until then what exists is participation.

And that is deliberate. Looking at partial results of a climate survey invites
conclusions about whoever answered first, and those are never a sample of anyone —
they are the most motivated, or whoever was at the tablet at nine in the morning.

### What you do see

**Collection progress**: how many people have responded, broken down by node, plus the
pace and an estimate of when it closes.

Progress is **not subject to the confidentiality threshold**. The threshold protects
survey results, not progress: knowing that three out of four people in a store responded
says nothing about what they answered.

### Each person's state

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Pending** | In the sample, not yet asked |
| **Assigned** | The email invitation has been sent |
| **Responding** | Answering right now at a tablet |
| **Completed** | Has responded |
| **Unavailable** | Left the company or was substituted |

> **«Unavailable» is what keeps collection from staying open forever.** Someone who has
> left will never respond; if they kept counting as pending, the study would never
> reach the end. Marking them removes them from the expected without inventing an
> answer they never gave.

## Finished

On finalization, results are **computed and stored**. From then on you have both indices
per branch, the breakdown by category, segments, and —from the second study onward— the
historical series.

Results are **persisted**, not recomputed every time you open the screen. That is what
makes a result published last year still say the same thing today, even if you have
since reorganized the tree or moved people between nodes.

## Summary

| | Draft | In progress | Finished |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edit scope and model | Yes | No | No |
| Edit that branch's questionnaire | Yes | No | No |
| See participation progress | — | Yes | Yes |
| See results | No | **No** | Yes |
| Counts toward history | No | No | Yes |

## If the product will not let you do something

Before hunting for a permission, check the state. The two usual causes are that the
study has already launched, or that your role does not write to that resource — and they
are easy to tell apart: if it is the state, the button is there but fails or warns; if it
is the role, **the button does not exist**. See
[Roles and permissions](/en/docs/roles).
